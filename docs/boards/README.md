# Boards: before and after

A board lays a collection out in columns, so you can move things along as they
progress. This page shows how the boards flow changed in the boards UX
overhaul, on desktop and on a phone, and why.

The short version: the old flow assumed you knew how boards worked inside. It
asked you to pick a "column set", put an unexplained "done" chip on every
column, and hid the fact that a board you made could be used again. The new
flow uses one word ("board"), shows you what you are about to get, and says
what each choice does at the moment you make it.

## Setting up a board

Choosing the Board view used to open a picker for a "column set". It now says what a board is in one sentence, and each template shows its columns before you pick it.

## Creating the board

The starting point you just chose is stated, not asked again. Columns can be recoloured and reordered here, and one sentence replaces the "done" chip on every row: "When a todo is ticked, move it to …".

## The board

Columns share the width of the screen, each has a coloured top edge and a caption line, and empty columns say what they are for. The board's name sits beside the collection's and opens its settings.

| --- | --- |

## Editing a column where you see it

Each column's menu renames it, changes its colour, moves it, deletes it, or
makes it the Finished column. Before, all of that meant a trip to Settings.

![The column menu](img/after-column-menu.png)

## Board settings

"It's a list — keep its todos off the Todos plan" is now a labelled switch, "Keep this collection's todos off the Todos page", with a line saying what it is for. A board shared with other collections says so, and offers a separate copy.

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |

## Using a board again

A board made for one collection is offered first to the next, with the collections that already use it. **Use** keeps the same columns as them; **Copy** gives this collection its own.

## Switching boards

"Move Movies to another set" became "Switch to a different board", with a preview of the board you are switching to and a plain account of where each column's items will go.

## On a phone

The toolbar fits the screen, a strip of column chips jumps between columns,
and sheets keep their buttons pinned while their content scrolls.
